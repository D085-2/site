# Arnold Air Society

## About
The Arnold Air Society (AAS) is a professional, honorary, service organization comprised of Air Force ROTC cadets and Academy midshipmen. At Detachment 085, we are the Doolittle Squadron, named in honor of General James "Jimmy" Doolittle—a pioneer in aviation and the legendary leader of the Doolittle Raid during World War II.

Our mission is to build high-caliber Air Force officers by fostering a culture of academic excellence, dedicated community service, and rigorous leadership development. The Doolittle Squadron provides cadets with an environment to challenge themselves, hone their professional skills, and grow alongside a highly motivated peer group dedicated to the highest standards of the United States Air Force.

## Activities

The Doolittle Squadron holds official meeting sessions every Friday at 1800 (6:00 PM). Beyond regular meetings, our various functional departments lead localized initiatives and professional development events throughout the academic year.

## Squadron initiatives and training events include:

- Community service projects and campus clean-ups
- Local volunteering and charitable fundraising campaigns
- Professional development and leadership workshops
- Comprehensive drill, ceremonies, and military customs training
- Air Force heritage and aviation history studies
- Critical thinking, problem-solving, and team-building exercises

Additionally, every year members have the opportunity to attend the National Conclave (NATCON). This premier national gathering brings AAS and Silver Wings chapters from across the country together to network with active-duty leadership, participate in national-level professional development, and vote on organization-wide policy and leadership.

## Candidacy Program

Membership in Arnold Air Society is earned, not given. Prospective members must successfully complete our rigorous 6-week candidacy program. The program is purposefully designed to test physical and mental stamina, build professional character, and prepare candidates for the demanding expectations of Air Force leadership.
